Unity Multiplayer with UDP: LAN Games Without Servers

Not every multiplayer game needs the internet. Party games, VR arcades, classrooms, and playtests all happen on local networks — where UDP gives you the lowest latency and zero hosting costs. Here's how it works.

Why UDP for real-time games

TCP guarantees delivery but waits and retries — great for files, terrible for a player position that's already outdated. UDP fires packets and moves on: lower latency, no head-of-line blocking, and if one position update is lost, the next one arrives milliseconds later anyway. That's why virtually all real-time games use UDP under the hood.

The two hard problems in LAN multiplayer

  1. Discovery. How do devices find each other without typing IP addresses? The answer is broadcast discovery — devices announce themselves on the local network and listen for others. Building this reliably across PC, mobile, and headsets is fiddly work.
  2. Messaging. Once connected, you need fast, simple send/receive for positions, inputs, and game events — without dragging in a heavyweight networking framework.

When LAN UDP is (and isn't) the answer

LAN UDP is perfect when players share a network: arcades, parties, offices, playtests. If your players are across the internet, you'll need relay servers or a hosted backend instead. But for the huge category of "people in the same room," nothing beats the simplicity — no server bills, no accounts, no latency to a distant data center.
