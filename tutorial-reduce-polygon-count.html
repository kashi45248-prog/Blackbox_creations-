How to Reduce Polygon Count in Unity

You imported a beautiful model and your frame rate tanked. It's 400,000 triangles — ten times what a game needs. Here's how mesh simplification (decimation) cuts the polygon count while keeping the look intact.

Why models arrive too heavy

Models from marketplaces, scans, and film-oriented artists are built for close-ups, not real-time rendering. A hero prop can carry 10–50x the triangles your game can afford. On PC you might get away with it; on mobile and Quest-class VR, every wasted triangle is battery and frames you're not getting back. The fix isn't buying simpler art — it's reducing what you have.

What mesh simplification does

Mesh simplification (also called decimation) is an algorithm that collapses edges and removes triangles where they contribute least — flat areas lose density first, silhouettes and detail are preserved longest. A good simplifier takes a 100k-triangle model to 10k and the difference is invisible at game-viewing distances. A bad one melts the model into a blob. Quality of the algorithm is everything.

What to look for in a simplifier

  1. Silhouette preservation. The outline of the model must survive aggressive reduction — that's what the eye reads first.
  2. Controllable reduction. You want a target (triangle count or percentage), not a mystery slider.
  3. Preview before committing. See the result, tune the settings, then apply — never a blind operation.
  4. Non-destructive workflow. Your original mesh should stay safe; simplification should produce a new asset.
